# Deformable Convolutions on Scaled MNIST

This notebook compares a small CNN with standard convolutions against the same CNN with two modulated deformable convolution layers, using PyTorch and `torchvision.ops.deform_conv2d`. Both models have five 3 × 3 convolutions with 32 channels, two max-pooling layers, global average pooling, and a linear classifier; in the deformable model, the last two convolutions predict an offset and a modulation weight for every kernel position from their input. The models are trained for 5 epochs on unmodified MNIST digits with Adam and a learning rate that is multiplied by 0.7 after every epoch, and they are tested on the test digits rescaled by 11 factors from 0.5 to 1.5. The notebook reports the best test accuracy over the 5 epochs and the training and test times of the last epoch. It also builds a multi-label COCO 2017 dataset, labeled with the 12 supercategories present in each image, which the models do not use.

## Results
The model with deformable convolutions reaches a best test accuracy of 92.06% on the rescaled digits, compared with 87.34% for the model with standard convolutions. It is also slower: its last training epoch takes 567.03 s instead of 73.84 s, and its last test pass takes 163.99 s instead of 49.96 s. The best accuracy is the maximum of the test accuracy over the 5 epochs, so it is selected on the test set.
